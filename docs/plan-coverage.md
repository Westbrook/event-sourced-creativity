## Adopted pilot controls

- Identity uses authenticated Sites or Cloudflare Access headers; the first private-site user is bootstrapped as admin and all authorization remains server-side.
- Generation uses server-side Responses planning with `store: false`; still
  images use the direct Image API or a separately governed image adapter, while
  ordered reference-backed video uses the approved Seedance contract. Images,
  stories, scenes, CSS-rendered slide decks, storyboards, and videos remain
  document-scoped durable outcomes. Media bytes remain in private object
  storage; receipts retain authenticated references and provider attribution,
  not base64 payloads.
- Budgets reserve capacity before an external call, settle against reported token usage, and fail closed when user or workspace limits are reached.
- Prompt/provider content is redacted after 30 days; events retain only non-sensitive receipts and references.
- Uploads require explicit ownership or license confirmation. Shares are
  read-only, expire after at most seven days, are immediately revocable, and
  authorize only their frozen document/project and exact owner-scoped media.
- The Phase 3 deterministic suite extracts dimensions, a k-means palette, dominant edge direction, mood, composition, material character, lighting, and spatial character from uploaded references. Each interpretive finding remains confidence-bearing evidence until the user explicitly adopts it as an editable decision. The frozen corpus gate must pass globally and per extractor at a correction rate of 10% or less.
- Phase 6 keeps generic inferred briefs behind three closed sessions and one dormant or unresolved thread. A directly declared or explicitly requested creator goal may instead surface its exact, evidence-linked unfinished work on the first return without enabling generic suggestions. Composition and item impressions append atomically. The human gate advanced with monitoring after the evidence floor; evidence continues to accrue and no metric is treated as an automatic pass threshold.
- Phase 7 uses a deterministic, versioned local embedding projection for the private pilot. Rebuilding writes only inferred family claims; vectors and corpus rankings remain disposable projections, family edges are individually correctable, and keeping a family still requires an authored collection event.
- Current local quick edits use explicit deterministic rules and a separate human-reviewed preview/acceptance. They do not call a model. Read-only related-work references are not evidence of model conditioning or learning, and no current cited-chat demonstration is certified here.
- The existing accepted-proposal corpus recognises the legacy `MutationProposed` and linked `ProposalAccepted` pair. The newer local-rule receipt grammar is not automatically covered by that classification; extending it requires explicit indexing and citation tests, not a coverage claim.
- Phase 8 derives deterministic trajectory slices before a run, records a hard envelope, groups falsifiable observations by run, and treats exhaustion as a planned terminal. Candidate artifacts remain in Scout report space until a user promotes one into an authored document block.
- Phase 9 keeps authored standing constraints and inferred rankings visibly separate. A pilot 60-day exponential half-life is versioned, every inferred claim exposes confidence/evidence/freshness, and revocation or retraction leaves the durable history inspectable as of any sequence. The critique ledger now follows `ObjectCritiqued → GenerationCompleted → ContextCompiled`, clusters evidence by model and adapter, and lists every contributing source block version without overstating association as singular causation.

## UI affordance audit

All visible buttons now do one of three explicit things: perform an event-backed action, navigate to a named destination, or remain disabled with the missing prerequisite in its label or tooltip. Project settings has a dedicated form; project state is a status rather than a false link. Collections use contextual create/rename dialogs, “File open document” records the document’s authored location, and collection navigation opens a filtered document view. Studio Library links land on Documents, Blocks, Outputs, or Families rather than a generic page. The project-wide Documents view appends `DocumentOpened` when switching active work, and reversible document removal tombstones the document while retaining its stream history for Undo. Canonical document-view visits are also witnessed as structured workflow evidence, including Focus state, so Re-entry can route through the strongest mode and Scout can locate view-specific progress or friction. This working-mode projection is explicitly excluded from aesthetic taste and generation context. “Use in open document” creates a cited authored copy and returns to Studio. Blocks expose focused previews plus outputs whose compile receipts cite them; outputs have focused context previews and distinguish the complete recent trail from the explicitly kept set; aesthetic families open all cited members in a lightbox. Compare controls are disabled until two current-document outputs exist. Ask Forma proposes across the closed block taxonomy instead of defaulting every request to Direction. The authored-block composer, project editor, reference viewers, and collection editor are dialog elements with backdrop dismissal. Undo finds the latest safe reversible authored event and redo targets the newly appended replay event. The document action bar remains sticky. Explicit-click instrumentation and the separate attention pilot are independently controllable. Attention capture is off by default, runs only while the page is visible, summarizes semantic context, document view, Focus state, and visible IDs without raw input content, discounts idle and page-exit endings, expires device detail after 14 days, and offers pause, review, and forget controls. Scout can use repeated friction episodes only as capped low-confidence support; Memory reports the signal but cannot turn it into authored or inferred taste on its own. Uploaded reference palettes remain read-only evidence and image sampling contains no arbitrary color input.

Creative Editor is the deliberately machinery-free document lens. It represents the complete document as a familiar tool palette, one-page creative plan, real generated-image preview, and contextual inspector. The user can add text and image material, edit copy, choose plain-language influence levels, include or pause an item, reorder, protect, duplicate, remove, undo, redo, and generate without seeing event types, IDs, handles, authority labels, provenance controls, sequence counts, or provider metadata. Those actions still use the same durable event vocabulary and projection as every other document view.

Blocks also support durable spatial targeting. Missing scope means the complete document; local scope can be a familiar or fuzzy named region, normalized AABB, subject-relative area, or normalized clipping polygon, with inside/outside polarity, feather, and hard, soft, or contextual falloff. The baseline Document exposes this as `Affects`; Context Board keeps it on the card; Guided Brief places it beside a saved answer; Reality Layers treats it like a layer mask; Adaptive Path asks where a resolved decision applies; Creative Editor includes it in the selected-item inspector. Every surface appends the same `BlockPropertyEdited` property, and compilation always receives an explicit normalized scope.

The affected-area preview is directly editable: rectangle mode draws a new normalized box and moves an existing box, freeform mode records a normalized clipping polygon, and natural-language entry authors a fuzzy custom region. Coordinate and polygon fields remain precision and accessibility fallbacks. Every block has an independent semantic role, `Include`/`Prefer`/`Avoid`/`Never` intent, and scope. Negative intent is visibly collected as a guardrail and compiler facts carry `effect: must_not_include`, so an avoided Subject, Mood, Palette, Material, or other role cannot be reinterpreted as a positive focus.

Creative Editor quick tools visibly preselect both role and intent. Every selected plan item has separate `Role in the plan` and `Intent` controls, allowing either dimension to be corrected through replayable property edits without deleting its content. The Avoid shortcut suggests Subject plus negative intent, legacy Exclusion blocks remain readable as general negative constraints, and the baseline Document and Reality Layers views expose the same intent property.

## Administrative scenarios

- Creative loop: extraction → authored correction → structured planning → `gpt-image-2` generation → private asset storage → compare → image export.
- Document evolution: project and document lifecycle → every authored block type
  and all five spatial-scope methods → reversible context, object, annotation,
  Library, artifact, and collection changes.
- Asynchronous return: session close → brief/scout report space → judgment → user acceptance → document promotion.
- Concurrent pressure: inert model proposal, commutative actions, stale validation, partial progress, explicit rejection.
- Family promotion: inferred grouping remains separate from the user-authored collection that keeps it.
- Taste & time: critique → versioned beliefs → authored preference → delayed resurfacing → revocation and forgetting.
- Attention & friction: visible-only dwell → weighted terminal actions → review and deletion.
- Operational safeguards: registry → reservation and settlement → structured failure → retention and phase-gate receipts.
- Canvas collaboration: project and membership → every shared source family → convergent text/register/stroke edits → explicit context-set generation → report-space vote/accept/reject/restore → re-homing, lock recovery, and reversible lifecycle.

Together the scenarios exercise the complete admitted event vocabulary, every authored block type, and the separately governed create/revoke receipts emitted by the trusted sharing service. Ordinary producer permissions never gain the ability to append share audits. Their deterministic clocks distinguish rapid user edits, agent handoffs, session interruptions, delayed resurfacing, branch abandonment, retention windows, document-view transitions, and collaborative Canvas handoffs. Complete tours additionally prove pointer-only intent declarations, exact creator review, first-interruption recovery, rights-bounded references, expiring read-only sharing, artifact-first reconstruction, and six distinct deliverables derived from one frozen creative context. Every selected step separately explains what happened, the concrete condition that triggered it, and how that event benefits the user; elapsed time remains supporting evidence rather than a causal explanation. The replay laboratory can build 1,000, 10,000, or 50,000 events, isolate any individual capability, reconstruct a hypothetical journey from a finished work product, and replay any projection to any sequence without writing fixtures to D1.
