## One finite operation

One terminal operation belongs to each genuine inventory. Repeating a read or a
consumption attempt cannot obtain another GET, copy or lifetime. The operation
captures the actual bucket, original signal and both the original monotonic and
retention deadlines. A later clock rollback cannot extend the captured ceiling.
Local identity checks are not fresh database proof: the full captured native proof
runs before R2, after byte/HEAD verification and before internal consumption.

The cumulative admission limits are one GET, one HEAD, two metadata captures
totalling at most 65,536 UTF-8 bytes, 65,536 stream reads, one SHA-256 inside
the unchanged PNG inspection, three complete inventory proof calls, one read
attempt and one consumption attempt. Input and the separate consumer copy each
have the existing 24,000,000-byte maximum. Inspection additionally reserves
115,108,864 scratch bytes: two maximum-sized compressed buffers and two 32 MiB
expanded buffers. These are conservative admission reservations, not measurements
of the JavaScript engine's heap or opaque native implementation overhead.
They do not prove that the combined worst case fits a Worker heap; no maximum-size
full-application workerd execution or performance qualification is claimed.

Stream length must equal the captured size, including rejection of underrun,
overrun, invalid chunks and an endless sequence of empty chunks. The unchanged
inspector preserves PNG CRC, chunk-order, encoding, expansion and scanline checks;
its maximum PNG chunk count is 4,096. Structural inspection does not establish
visual quality or content acceptance.

All awaits race the same original remaining lifetime. Stop is terminal. Late GET
bodies are cancelled best effort, with rejected cleanup promises observed, without
waiting for an unresponsive cancellation. JavaScript timers cannot preempt
synchronous work or physically cancel native hashing, inspection or a D1 query.
An input used by pending hashing/inspection remains owned and unchanged until the
actual consumer settles, even if foreground authority has already expired. Buffer
retirement does not pretend that non-cancellable work ended.

The observation has no synchronous raw-byte getter. Internal consumption first
repeats the full native proof and receives a separate, one-shot owned buffer. That
copy remains allocated until the actual callback settles, then retires along with
the operation. No observation or callback grants an R2 write, claim lease or new
identity permission.
